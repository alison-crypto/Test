## 1. Photo standards
- **Camera:**
  - Tripod at a marked floor spot ~2.5–3 m away. Same phone, same lens (use 2x, not the close-up wide lens, which bulges the middle of the frame).
  - Camera at mid-torso (navel to lower sternum), lens level.
  - High angle → shoulders look bigger, legs shorter. Low angle → the opposite.
  - Timer or burst. Same framing, head to feet. [M] (clinical photography standards, refs 42)
- **Light:**
  - Same place, same light, same time of day. Diffuse frontal light, no flash, plain neutral background.
  - Overhead or side light exaggerates definition. This is the #1 source of fake "progress".
- **Poses:**
  - Relaxed front, both sides and back. Feet on a floor marker, arms slightly away from the body, normal exhale, eyes on a fixed point.
  - Flexed (front double biceps, side, back double biceps): use only for muscle-shape notes.
- **Conditions:** morning, fasted, after the toilet, before training, same weekday as the weigh-in and tape, same shorts at the same waistband height.
- **Invalid if:**
  - Distance, height, lens or light changed.
  - Pump, a high-carb or high-salt day, alcohol, or illness.
  - Tan or hair removal.
  - Different breathing or stance, waistband moved, filters/HDR/beauty mode, or different crop.
  - 2 or more failures → "not comparable".

## 2. Visual body fat
- **One trained rater:** total error ~2.3 points vs hydrostatic weighing in lean men. Agreement between raters was poor. Self-estimates are worse (Eckerson 1992) [M].
- **AI 2D-photo methods:** MAE ~2.3 points vs DXA (Majmudar 2022) [M].
- **Practical error:** ±3–4 points (95%) for one photo. DXA itself is off by 1–3 points.
- **Cues for men [E]:**

| Body fat | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| ~10–12% | Upper and usually lower abs, serratus/obliques, abdominal veins, delt–arm and quad separation. |
| ~15% | Upper abs faint in good light, blurred waist lines, some flank softness, shape without separation. |
| ~20% | No abs, smooth rounded midsection, mild lower-belly pooch, love handles from behind, soft chest edges. |
| ~25% | Waist ≈ or > hips, overhang above the waistband, chest fat, fuller face and neck, back folds when relaxed. |

- **Limits:** fat distribution (android/visceral), muscle mass (looks leaner), lighting and pump (several points), age and ethnicity.
- **Report:** a range plus a direction. Never let the photo estimate override the tape or scale trend.

## 3. Scale, tape and ratios
- **Consumer BIA vs DXA:**
  - Fat-mass error −2.2 to −4.4 kg. Weight is accurate (0–0.3 kg) (Frija-Masson 2021) [M].
  - 15 devices vs a 4-compartment model: day-to-day precision 0–0.49 points, absolute bias −3.5 to +11.7 points, change-tracking error 1.7–2.6 points over 12–16 weeks (Siedler/Tinsley 2023) [M].
- **What shifts BIA:**
  - Food and drink: about +1 point fat.
  - Water just before: can be read as fat.
  - Glycogen, sodium, alcohol, training the day before, skin temperature, creatine.
  - Carb-depleted → BIA over-reads fat. Refeed → under-reads.
- **Standard conditions:** on waking, after urinating, before food, coffee or exercise, dry bare feet, hard floor, same scale and mode.
- **What to trust:**
  - Use the 2–4 week trend.
  - Weight is the best signal.
  - BIA "muscle mass" means lean mass + water, not muscle.
  - Visceral rating (Tanita-style): 1–12 = healthy, 13+ = excess. It is a model estimate; use only its direction, together with waist.
- **Tape (ISAK):**
  - Use a non-stretch tape, flat and level, no skin compression, read at the end of a normal exhale.
  - Measure 2× per site. Take a 3rd if they differ by > 1%. Record the mean (or median of 3).
  - Use fixed landmarks (e.g. waist at the navel; thigh X cm above the kneecap). Never switch sites.
  - Error: skilled ≈ 1%. Self-measured waist ICC ≥ 0.87, but the minimum real change is ~1–1.5 cm (careful, same person) to ~3 cm (literature) (Verweij 2013) [M].
- **US Navy formula (neck + waist + height):**
  - SEE ~3.5 points. Averages ~2.5 points under DXA, ±3.7.
  - Over-estimates lean men, under-estimates fatter men (Hodgdon 1984; Front Physiol 2022) [M].
  - A thick neck from training lowers it. Use it for the trend only.
- **Ratios [S]:**
  - Waist ÷ height ≥ 0.50 = raised cardiometabolic risk; 0.40–0.50 is healthy (Ashwell 2012, 2016).
  - Waist ÷ hip ≥ 0.90 in men = increased risk (WHO 2008).
  - WHO waist for men: ≥ 94 cm increased risk, ≥ 102 cm substantially increased.

## 4. Posture
- **Forward head:**
  - The craniovertebral angle (CVA) from a true side photo is the most reliable non-X-ray measure. Smartphone ICC 0.87–0.99. CVA < ~48–50° is often called "forward head" [M].
- **Anterior pelvic tilt:**
  - Visual rating is unreliable (inter-rater κ 0.16).
  - 85% of pain-free men have it; average ~9–10°, range 3–18° (Herrington 2011) [M].
  - A photo can't separate tilt from glute or belly fat.
- **Scapular winging/dyskinesis:**
  - Visual κ 0.48–0.61 even on video. Common in people with no symptoms.
  - Marked one-sided winging can be a nerve problem → physio or doctor.
- **Asymmetry:** shoulder or pelvis height differences < 1–1.5 cm are normal plus photo error. Confirm over several weeks.
- **Exercises:**
  - For forward head: chin tucks, scapular retraction and lower-trap work, pec stretch, thoracic extension. Large CVA improvement, moderate pain reduction (Sheikhhoseini 2018; Sepehri 2024) [S].
  - For lifters: rows 1.5–2 : 1 to presses, face pulls, prone Y/T, serratus work, thoracic mobility, hip-flexor stretch + glute strength.
- **Debate:** posture → pain links are weak; no consensus that posture causes back or neck pain (Swain 2020) [S]. Frame fixes as strength, appearance and end-range control, never as fear.

## 5. Proportions and asymmetry
- **Ratios to track:**
  - Waist-to-chest is the main body cue in attractiveness ratings of men (Fan 2005) [M].
  - A shoulder : waist ≈ 1.6 "Adonis" target is a convention, not evidence.
  - Track shoulders ÷ waist and chest ÷ waist. Both improve from fat loss plus delt and lat growth.
- **Photo cues:** delt width and cap, rear delts, lat spread, upper chest, arms vs forearms, quad sweep, hamstring/glute tie-in, calves vs thighs.
  - HYROX athletes often lag in upper chest and side delts.
- **Asymmetry:**
  - Strength: 10–15% is the usual flag, but the cutoff is arbitrary (Bishop 2018/2021).
  - Girth: ≤ 2% (0.5–1 cm) is normal (dominant side bigger).
  - Flag only > 3% or > 1.5 cm across 3+ sessions, or a sudden change with pain or weakness → refer.

## 6. Rates and nutrition
- **Rate of loss:**
  - 0.5–1.0% body weight per week keeps muscle; slower when leaner (Helms 2014) [S].
  - Elite athletes at 0.7%/wk gained lean mass and strength; at 1.4%/wk they didn't (Garthe 2011) [S].
- **Deficit size:** ≥ ~500 kcal/day stopped resistance-training lean gains, though strength still rose (Murphy & Koehler 2022) [S]. Use 300–500 kcal/day at most.
- **Recomposition:** possible with progressive lifting, 1.6–2.4 g/kg protein, maintenance or a small deficit, and good sleep. Most likely in returning or higher-fat trainees (Barakat 2020) [M]. Signs: waist down, weight stable, lifts up.
- **Protein:**
  - Lean gains plateau at ~1.6 g/kg (upper CI ~2.2) (Morton 2018) [S].
  - In a deficit: 1.6–2.4 g/kg (Hector & Phillips 2018). Longland 2016: 2.4 g/kg gained +1.2 kg lean vs 1.2 g/kg [S].
  - Spread it over 4–5 meals of 0.3–0.4 g/kg, plus one before bed.
- **Carbs:**
  - 3–5 g/kg light days, 5–7 moderate, 6–10 heavy (Thomas 2016) [S].
  - "Fuel for the work required": high around intervals, threshold work and sims; lower on easy and rest days (Impey 2018) [M].
  - In a deficit, take calories from fat and from easy-day carbs.
- **Fat:** ≥ 20–25% of energy.
- **Sleep:** at the same deficit, 5.5 h vs 8.5 h of sleep gave −55% fat loss and +60% lean loss (Nedeltcheva 2010) [M, small RCT]. Under ~6.5 h on several nights → shrink the deficit.
- **Low energy availability (REDs, IOC 2023):**
  - Lowers testosterone, metabolic rate and bone markers. There is no validated male cutoff [S].
  - Signs: low libido, no morning erections, low mood, persistent fatigue, more illness or injury.
  - Extreme contest prep crushed testosterone (Rossow 2013, case) [weak]. A moderate cut from ~21% to ~16–17% is unlikely to do that.

## 7. Minimum detectable change
| Measure | Noise | Review window |
|---|---|---|
| Scale weight | ±1–2 kg day to day; weekly cycle ~0.35% (Turicchi 2020) | 7-day averages; trust the direction after 2–3 weeks |
| BIA fat % | Change-tracking error 1.7–2.6 points | 4-week trend only; < 2–3 points over 8–12 weeks is uncertain |
| Waist | ~1–1.5 cm (careful self) to 3 cm | ≥ 2 cm over 2–4 weeks is probably real |
| Limb girths | ~0.5–1 cm | 4–8 weeks; they fall with fat loss even when muscle is kept |
| Photos | ±2–4 points | 3–4 weeks apart; needs ~2–4 kg fat or 3–5 cm waist to be clearly visible [E] |

- **Expected at ~0.5–0.6 kg/wk:** about 0.5–1 cm/wk off the waist. Confirmed on the tape by weeks 3–4, visible in photos by weeks 4–6.

## 8. Decision rules and HYROX [E, from the evidence above]
1. **Waist down, 7-day weight stable (±0.25 kg/wk), performance fine** → recomposition working. Keep everything. Add 100–150 kcal if recovery suffers.
2. **Weight −0.5–1%/wk, waist down, performance fine** → on track, no change.
3. **Weight down > 1%/wk for 2+ weeks, OR key lifts down > 5%, OR easy-run HR up at the same pace, OR poor sleep/mood/libido** → +150–250 kcal/day, mostly carbs around key sessions. Check load and sleep. Screen for low energy availability.
4. **Weight and waist flat 2–3 weeks (logging accurate)** → −150–250 kcal/day from fat or easy-day carbs, or +1,500–2,000 steps/day. Not more hard sessions.
5. **Weight up and waist up** → check logging, alcohol and weekends. Rule out water (creatine, sodium, a hard session the day before).
6. **BIA fat up, tape and photos better** → BIA artifact, ignore it.
7. **Limb girth down, lifts holding** → fat loss, not muscle loss.
8. **Persistent L/R gap (> 3% girth or > 10–15% strength, 3+ sessions)** → unilateral work, weaker side first, match reps.
9. **Posture flag consistent across weeks** → 2–3 optional posture-strength sets per session. Pain, numbness or marked winging → physio.

**HYROX:**
- **What predicts finish time:**
  - Running is ~half of race time.
  - Strongest correlates: VO₂max (ρ −0.71), endurance volume (−0.68), body fat % (+0.67).
  - Grip, muscle-mass % and resistance volume were not meaningful (Brandt 2025, n=11) [M].
  - ~80% of the race is spent above 90% HRmax.
- **Body mass:** the energy cost of running is ~1 kcal/kg/km, so losing fat lowers the cost of 8 km plus burpee broad jumps and lunges. Sleds favour mass and leg strength, so don't cut at the expense of legs; keep 1–2 heavy lower-body sessions a week.
- **Timeline:** moderate deficit until ~2 weeks out → maintenance → taper. Carbs ~6–8 g/kg in the last 24–36 h. Never cut weight in race week.
- **Interference:** keep hard lifting and hard running ≥ 6 h apart or on different days. Cut fat through diet, not extra conditioning.

## 9. Safety, language and privacy
- **Muscle dysmorphia:** a body dysmorphic disorder specifier; ~7% of male students screened positive. Body checking (mirror, pinching, repeat measuring, frequent weighing) is linked to eating disorders, depression and PED use (Cunningham 2017; MBCQ) [M].
- **Red flags → pause appearance coaching and refer:**
  - Distress or guilt over food or missed sessions, rigid rituals, training through injury.
  - Checking photos or tape more than weekly; wanting > 1%/wk loss; < ~25 kcal/kg intake; binge/compensate cycles.
  - PEDs, diuretics, water or sodium cutting.
  - Social withdrawal around food or the gym; "I look small / disgusting"; fixation on one body part despite progress.
- **Language:**
  - Do: neutral, specific, functional words; credit the process; give error ranges.
  - Avoid: "bad", "ugly", "gross", "flaw", "problem area", "skinny-fat"; comparisons with other people; body-fat numbers stated as fact; fear-based posture claims; praising extreme leanness.
- **Refer:**
  - Physician: suspected eating disorder or muscle dysmorphia, PEDs, persistent low-energy/low-testosterone signs, unexplained loss.
  - Psychologist or eating-disorder specialist: suspected eating disorder or muscle dysmorphia.
  - Dietitian: individual fuelling.
  - Physio: pain, nerve signs, marked winging, new asymmetry with weakness.
- **Privacy:** body photos plus health data are sensitive (PIPEDA / GDPR Art. 9). Store the minimum, private and owner-only. Never in a repo, artifact or shared report. Delete on request.
