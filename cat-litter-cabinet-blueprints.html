Verify before you cut the doors

Every dimension on these sheets comes from the published box size, except one: the height of the cat holes. Dry-fit a litter box in its bay, stand the door (part E) up against the opening, and mark the hole so its bottom edge sits level with the bottom of the box's own entry. The 5½″ shown on D-3 is a starting point, not a measurement.

Cut the carcass parts with confidence — they are all independent of this. The doors are the last thing you need to commit to.

General notes

  1. Cutting desk. Ask for the rips first — five in total — then the crosscuts. Their panel saw holds about ±⅛″, which is fine here: nothing is inset, the doors are full overlay, and the block overhangs both ends. Most stores won't cut anything narrower than about 2–3″, which is why parts H are 1×4 pine rather than ripped plywood.
  2. Divider layout. Measuring from the left end of the bottom deck (part C), the dividers land at 24″ and 44¼″. That gives clear bays of 24″ / 19½″ / 24″.
  3. Joinery. Glue and countersunk #8 × 1⅝″ screws through the faces, filled and painted. No dados, no pocket-hole jig.
  4. Service holes, cut before the back goes on. One 2″ cord hole low in each litter bay — both boxes need power — and two 2″ vent holes high in each bay. Enclosed litter without airflow just concentrates the smell.
  5. Doors. Three full-overlay cup hinges each; two is not enough for 30″ of ¾″ plywood. Hinge them on the outer edges so both doors swing away from the centre bay.
  6. Fixing the top. Screw up through the rails into the block with the rail holes drilled ¼″ oversized, or use figure-8 fasteners. Solid wood that wide has to be free to move seasonally or it will split.
  7. Waste drawer. Before you settle each box's position, check that its waste drawer clears the door opening — otherwise you'll be dragging the whole unit out every time.
