# Cat Litter Cabinet — Build Plan & Home Depot Cut List

Two automatic litter boxes side by side, front-facing cat entrances, butcher block top,
72" max overall. Built cheap and simple: one plywood box, three bays, two doors.

---

## 1. What we're working around

**Litter box (Cavrtbany, Large):** 53.3 L × 49.8 W × 66 H cm = **21" × 19 3/4" × 26" tall**

The 26" height is what drives the whole design. That is a tall box, so everything has to
live on one level — there is no room for an upper shelf section like the inspiration photo
without the cabinet ending up 40"+ tall. Single tier it is, which also makes it much cheaper
and faster to build.

Two of them, so the carcass needs two bays that each clear 21" × 19 3/4" on the floor and 26"
of headroom, plus room to slide the unit out for servicing.

---

## 2. Final dimensions

| | |

| **Overall (with top)** | **72" W × 25" D × 32 1/2" H** |
| Plywood carcass | 70 1/2" W × 22 1/2" D × 31" H |
| Butcher block top | 72" × 25" × 1 1/2" (6 ft stock — no cutting needed) |
| Litter bays (2) | 24" W × 22 1/4" D × 29 1/2" H clear |
| Centre bay | 19 1/2" W, open front, 2 shelves |
| Clearance around box | 3" side-to-side, 2 1/2" front-to-back, 3 1/2" overhead |

The block is 1 1/2" longer than the carcass so it overhangs **3/4" at each end**, and sits
flush at the back for **2 1/4" of front overhang**. That hides any slop in your end cuts and
looks intentional. Total stays exactly at your 72" limit.

**Roughly $530 – $790 all in.** The butcher block is over half of it — price that first, it
swings the budget more than anything else.

**Plywood vs MDF:** use **plywood for the carcass**. MDF is cheaper and paints beautifully,
but it swells permanently if it ever gets wet, and a litter cabinet is the worst possible
place for that. If you want the ultra-flat painted look, a fair compromise is plywood box +
MDF doors — the doors stay dry.

**Tools you'll need at home:** drill/driver, countersink bit, **35mm Forstner bit** (~$15, for
the hinge cups), jigsaw (cat holes), 2" hole saw (cord + vent holes), tape, square, 2 clamps,
sandpaper, iron (for edge banding).

## 4. Cut list — hand this to the Home Depot cutting desk

All 3/4" parts come out of **2 sheets**. Ask for the **rips first**, then the crosscuts.

### Sheet 1 — 3/4" plywood

> **Rip the full sheet into two strips 22 1/2" wide.** (Leaves a ~3" waste strip.)

| Part | Qty | Size | From |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** — Side panels | 2 | 22 1/2" × 30 1/4" | Strip 1 |
| **B** — Divider | 1 | 22 1/2" × 29 1/2" | Strip 1 |
| **C** — Bottom deck | 1 | 22 1/2" × 69" | Strip 2 |

*Strip 1 usage: 30 1/4 + 30 1/4 + 29 1/2 = 90" of 96". Strip 2: 69" of 96".*

### Sheet 2 — 3/4" plywood

> **Rip the full sheet into one strip 22 1/2" wide and one strip 25" wide.**

| Part | Qty | Size | From |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B** — Divider | 1 | 22 1/2" × 29 1/2" | 22 1/2" strip |
| **D** — Centre shelves | 2 | 22 1/2" × 19 3/8" | 22 1/2" strip |
| **E** — Doors | 2 | 25" × 30 1/8" | 25" strip |

*22 1/2" strip usage: 68 1/4" of 96". 25" strip: 60 1/4" of 96" — leftover is enough for a

### Sheet 3 — 1/4" plywood (back)

> **Rip one strip 31" wide**, then crosscut:

| Part | Qty | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **F** — Back, left | 1 | 31" × 45 3/8" |
| **G** — Back, right | 1 | 31" × 25 1/8" |

The seam lands dead centre on the right-hand divider, so both pieces are supported.

### Pine (cut at home, easy crosscuts)

| Part | Qty | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **H** — Top rails | 2 | 1×4 × 69" |

### Notes for the cutting desk

- Home Depot's panel saw holds about **± 1/8"**. That's fine here — nothing is inset,
  the doors are full overlay, and the block overhangs the ends.
- Some stores cap free cuts (often 2–3 free, then ~$1 each). This plan is **5 rips + 11
  crosscuts**, so budget ~$15 and ask nicely.
- They usually **won't cut anything narrower than ~2–3"**, which is exactly why the top rails
  are 1×4 pine instead of ripped plywood. Don't ask them for the 3 1/2" strips.
- Ask them to keep the **factory edge** as your reference edge on the long rips.
- The 6 ft butcher block needs **no cutting at all** — it's already 72".

## 5. Assembly

Simple butt joints, glue + countersunk screws through the faces. It's getting painted, so
fill the screw heads and nobody will know. No dados, no pocket-hole jig required.

1. **Mark the divider positions on the bottom deck (C).** From the left end of the deck:
   divider 1 at **24"**, divider 2 at **44 1/4"**. (Deck is 69" long and sits *between* the
   sides, so bays measure 24" / 19 1/2" / 24" of clear space.)
2. **Screw the two sides (A) onto the ends of the deck.** The deck goes *between* the sides,
   with its underside flush with the bottom edges of the sides. Glue the joint and drive
   3 screws per side through the outside face of the side panel into the deck's edge.
   Carcass is now 70 1/2" wide, and you have 29 1/2" of clear height above the deck.
3. **Screw the two dividers (B)** down through the deck from underneath (3 screws each),
   square to the sides.
4. **Lay the two rails (H) flat on the top edges** of the sides and dividers — one flush at
   the front, one flush at the back. Screw down into every panel. Laying them flat instead of
   on edge is what preserves the full 29 1/2" of door opening. The carcass is now rigid.
5. **Cut the service holes before the back goes on** (much easier):
   - **Cord hole:** 2" hole saw, back panel, one per litter bay, 3" up from the deck in the
     outside corner. Both boxes need power — plan on a power bar behind the cabinet.
   - **Vent holes:** two more 2" holes near the top of each litter bay's back panel. Enclosed
     litter needs airflow or the smell just concentrates.
6. **Nail or screw the back (F, G) on** with 3/4" brads or 5/8" screws every 8".
7. **Shelves (D):** screw scrap plywood cleats to the centre-bay dividers and drop the shelves
   on. Suggested heights: 10" and 20" above the deck.
8. **Cat holes in the doors (E).** 8" wide × 9" tall, centred left-to-right on each door.
   Round the corners generously with a jigsaw and sand the edges smooth — this is where the
   cats rub every single day.
   > ⚠️ **Measure your box's own opening first.** Set the *bottom* of the cat hole level with
   > the *bottom* of the litter box's entry, roughly **5 1/2" up from the bottom of the door**.
   > Dry-fit the box in the bay, hold the door in place, and mark it off the real box before
   > you cut. This is the one measurement I can't give you from the spec sheet.
9. **Hang the doors** — 3 cup hinges per door (30" of 3/4" plywood is too much for 2). Bore
   the 35mm cups 3" from top, 3" from bottom, one centred. Doors are full overlay: 1/2" onto
   the outer side panel, 1/2" onto the divider.
10. **Edge band** the exposed front edges of the sides, dividers, deck and shelves. Iron on,
    trim with a utility knife, sand. Skip this on the doors if you're using MDF.
11. **Prime and paint** everything before the top goes on. Two coats, and prime the inside of
    the litter bays too — sealed surfaces wipe clean.
12. **Butcher block on last.** Flush at the back, 3/4" overhang each end. Screw up through the
    rails into the block from underneath — **drill the rail holes 1/4" oversized** (or use
    figure-8 tabletop fasteners) so the solid wood can move seasonally without splitting.
    Finish the block with mineral oil or wipe-on poly before you set it down.
13. **Felt pads** on the bottom corners.

## 6. Things worth doing while you're in there

- **Line the bay floors.** Peel-and-stick vinyl tile or a cheap boot tray under each box.
  Tracked litter and the odd miss come off a hard surface in seconds; painted plywood
  eventually stains.
- **Check the waste drawer travel.** Automatic boxes pull their waste drawer out the front.
  Set each box so the drawer clears the door opening, or you'll be dragging the whole unit
  out every time.
- **Litter refill.** With 3 1/2" of headroom you can't tip a bag in from above — plan on
  sliding the box forward, or keep a scoop and a jug in the centre bay for topping up.
- **Two cords, one bar.** Drill a 2" pass-through in each divider if you want a single power
  bar in the centre bay feeding both sides.
- **A cheap upgrade later:** the leftover 25" × 35" piece from Sheet 2 makes a third door if
  you decide you'd rather close in the centre bay than leave it open.
